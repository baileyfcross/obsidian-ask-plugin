# Algebraic Simplification

Algebraic simplification transforms an expression into a more useful equivalent form. Common steps include applying exponent laws, distributing factors, suppressing parentheses, arranging terms, and [[Combining Like Terms]].

Simpler does not always mean shorter in every context. Expansion may expose like terms, while [[Polynomial Factorization]] may expose zeros or permit cancellation. Each step must be justified by an arithmetic property and preserve the expression for all permitted variable values. A cancelled denominator factor still leaves the original restriction that the factor cannot equal zero.
